Before you begin

A few useful answers.

You can start without sharing your financial details.

What does the Money Confidence Check measure?

It reflects how you rate 25 statements across Money Awareness, Financial Safety, Investing Confidence, Decision Confidence, and Future Readiness. It does not test factual knowledge, financial health, or investment suitability.

Why does the check use 1 to 5?

It follows the scale in Namita’s original scorecard: never, rarely, sometimes, usually, and always true. Your total can range from 25 to 125. Some statements describe whether you have something today; choose the response that feels closest. Treat the outcome as a conversation starter.

Can I see my result without subscribing?

Yes. Both online checks show results immediately. All three original PDFs are available without an email address. Newsletter signup is optional.

What happens to my answers?

They stay in this page’s memory and are cleared on reload or when you leave. No answers or scores are sent with newsletter signup. You can download a text copy of your result to your own device.

Is the Money Reset a psychological assessment?

No. It is a new six-question reflection exercise inspired by the workbook. It points to themes you selected, such as fear, learned beliefs, unclear next steps, or lack of routine. More than one can apply.

What is available in Hinglish?

Both online checks, their results, and the email drafts are available in Roman-script Hinglish. The main website and the original PDFs are in English.

Do I need an investment account for the Money Date?

No. Start with accounts and documents you already have. Skip an investing section that does not apply. Learning or locating a document can be your one money move.

Is there a paid community or advisory service?

Not at launch. The newsletter and Namita’s social channels are the places to learn and ask general questions. This website does not offer personalized advice or portfolio management.
